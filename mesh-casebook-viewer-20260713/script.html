2. MANNA CEA

Company introduction

Let me briefly introduce MANNA CEA. MANNA CEA was founded in 2013. We have supplied turnkey controlled-environment agriculture solutions to around ten countries, including greenhouse, hydroponic, vertical farming, and smart farm projects.

In the past, we were known more for aquaponics and turnkey farm construction. Recently, however, we are focusing our company direction more clearly around MESH.

3. MESH

MESH positioning

MESH is our AI-ready farm automation and management platform. It connects sensors, control modules, existing equipment, farm operation records, and dashboard workflows, so that growers can monitor, control, and gradually use data for better decision-making.

The key point is that MESH is not only a climate controller. We see it more as a modular operating layer for farms.

Depending on the site, MESH can start as simple monitoring, a control base, a retrofit integration layer, or a phased automation platform.

5. AI / Data

Connect MESH to research value

For today’s discussion, I think the most relevant topic may be how MESH can create structured farm data for AI and computer vision applications.

In many farms, sensor data, control logs, crop observations, irrigation events, and grower notes are separated. Our goal with MESH is to connect these layers into a usable operational dataset.

This could be useful for AI-based greenhouse operation, crop monitoring, anomaly detection, image-based crop analysis, and eventually decision-support models.

6. Computer Vision

Questions for Dan

We are also interested in image-based crop analysis, but we are still careful about how to apply it practically. From our perspective, computer vision becomes more useful when image data is connected with environmental data, irrigation data, growth stage, and actual grower actions.

So I would be very interested in your view: what kind of image data or greenhouse operation data is most useful for research in crop monitoring or AI-based cultivation support?

7. Collaboration

Possible cooperation

I think there could be several possible ways to collaborate. One possibility is technical exchange: understanding how Wageningen approaches computer vision and AI for greenhouse horticulture, while we share field-side data collection and automation experience from Korea and overseas projects.

Another possibility is a small pilot or research collaboration, where MESH is used as a data collection and operation layer, and the data can support AI or computer vision validation.

A third possibility could be Korea-Netherlands knowledge exchange, especially because you also have a connection with South Korea.

8. If Asked

If he asks: Is MESH a climate computer?

That is a good question. We do not position MESH as a direct replacement for established climate computers in every case.

In some sites, MESH can control devices directly. But in retrofit or advanced greenhouse sites, MESH can also work as a complementary layer: collecting data, connecting additional sensors, organizing operation records, and preparing the farm data for AI-based analysis.

So the role depends on the site. Sometimes MESH controls. Sometimes it monitors. Sometimes it integrates. The important point is that it can start small and expand after validation.
