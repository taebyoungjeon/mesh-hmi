Subject: RE: Thank you and follow ups - strawberry smart farm business case

Dear Ms Riny,

Thank you for your questions, and please accept my apologies for the late reply.

We reviewed the earlier strawberry smart farm business case again. The initial report was prepared as a preliminary feasibility view based on the 4,000 m2 greenhouse concept. Since then, we have also been reviewing a more compact indoor vertical strawberry model, so I would like to answer your questions with that context as well.

1. Selling price used in the revenue forecast

The revenue forecast was not based on final retail price. It was intended to reflect a premium wholesale / reseller price to modern retail, HORECA, or distributor channels.

The assumed price range in the forecast was approximately USD 11.1-22.2/kg depending on the harvest month. This is materially below the surveyed Korean imported strawberry retail benchmark in Indonesia, so the model assumes that the farm sells to a reseller / trader / offtaker, not directly to the final consumer.

2. Revenue size vs market demand

Your reading is broadly correct if we compare the forecast revenue against the 2021 strawberry import market size used in the report.

Annual revenue of approximately USD 478k is about 13.8% of the USD 3.46m import market reference. If compared against the wider Indonesian strawberry market value in the report, the share would be lower. Therefore, we see the first project as a phased premium-market entry, not as an immediate large-scale market replacement.

3. Allowance for failed harvest, reject, waste, return or unsold product

The simple profitability summary in the previous report did not fully model all commercial losses as a separate line item. It was a preliminary gross operating view.

For a more bankable model, we recommend including a loss allowance. For the first operating cycle, we would use 10-15% for reject, grade loss, unsold product and learning-period loss. After stabilization, this may be reduced to around 5-8%, depending on the offtake contract, cold-chain performance, and farm operating discipline.

For indoor vertical production, crop failure risk can be lower than conventional production because climate, irrigation and hygiene are more controlled. However, it should not be assumed as zero.

4. CAPEX scope

Yes. The previously stated CAPEX of approximately USD 1.07m excludes land.

It also excludes, or needs separate confirmation for, international freight, customs duties, import taxes, local permitting, and any site-specific civil works or utility upgrades. The exact split should be finalized after the site and facility format are confirmed.

5. Items supplied by MANNA CEA vs locally sourced

MANNA CEA can provide the core cultivation technology package, including cultivation system design, strawberry growing system, nutrient supply configuration, environmental-control logic, MESH monitoring / control layer, commissioning, training, and cultivation know-how.

Items that can normally be locally sourced include civil works, building or greenhouse shell, local electrical work, plumbing, water tanks, general HVAC installation, local construction labor, and part of the consumables / packaging. For quality-critical items such as cultivation racks, LED specification, fertigation equipment, sensors, control gateway, and cultivation recipe, we recommend that MANNA CEA either supplies them directly or approves the local supplier specification.

6. Cost of strawberry production in Indonesia vs Korea

On an indicative basis, Indonesia should have a lower production cost than Korea if the farm is operated with local labor and optimized local procurement. However, the difference is not simply proportional to labor cost, because Indonesia requires stronger cooling and dehumidification control.

For an indoor vertical strawberry model, our preliminary view is:

- Indonesia stabilized production cost: approximately USD 8-12/kg
- Korea stabilized production cost: approximately USD 11-16/kg

The main reasons are:

- Labor cost is materially lower in Indonesia.
- Some local construction and installation costs can be lower in Indonesia.
- Electricity tariff may be lower than Korea, but Indonesia's tropical climate increases cooling and dehumidification load.
- Korea has stronger strawberry seedling, grower and maintenance infrastructure, so the early-stage technical risk is lower in Korea.
- Indonesia may have higher initial learning cost, import dependency for key components, and seedling / variety licensing complexity.

Therefore, our practical conclusion is that Indonesia can become more cost-competitive than Korea after stabilization, but the first pilot should be used to validate yield, cooling cost, loss rate, and market price before scaling.

For the indoor vertical strawberry model, a realistic production target is around 15-16 kg/m2/year based on floor area, assuming about 19-20 plants/m2 and approximately 0.6 kg/plant over an extended indoor production period. This is not an impossible production target, but it should be validated locally through a pilot.
